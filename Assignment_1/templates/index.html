Study of Two Datasets from Different Domains

1. UCI Heart Disease Dataset Domain: Healthcare

Source: UCI Machine Learning Repository (donated by Robert Detrano, M.D., Ph.D., 1988); records collected from the Cleveland Clinic Foundation (USA), Hungarian Institute of Cardiology (Budapest), V.A. Medical Center (Long Beach), and University Hospital (Zurich/Basel, Switzerland).

Purpose

The dataset is used to build classification models that predict the presence or absence of heart disease in a patient from routine clinical and diagnostic measurements. It supports research on early, low-cost, non-invasive screening tools that can assist doctors in identifying at-risk patients before a full cardiac work-up is performed. It is one of the most widely used benchmark datasets in medical machine learning research.

Theory / Description

The full repository contains 76 attributes collected across four hospitals, but nearly all published research uses a reduced, standard subset of 14 attributes. The most common version (Cleveland subset) has 303 patient records, while the combined multi-hospital version has 920 records. The target variable indicates the presence (1) or absence (0) of heart disease, making this a binary classification problem. The dataset contains a mix of categorical (e.g. chest pain type, sex) and numerical (e.g. cholesterol, resting blood pressure) features.

2. CIFAR-10 Dataset Domain: Computer Vision

Source: Created by Alex Krizhevsky, with Vinod Nair and Geoffrey Hinton, Canadian Institute for Advanced Research (CIFAR) / University of Toronto (2009); a labeled subset of the larger 80 Million Tiny Images dataset.

Purpose

CIFAR-10 is a benchmark dataset used to design, train, and evaluate supervised image classification algorithms — from classical CNNs to modern attention-based and interpretable architectures. Because the images are small and the dataset is balanced, it is widely used to quickly prototype and compare new deep learning and machine learning architectures before scaling to larger, real-world image datasets.

Theory / Description

The dataset consists of 60,000 colour images of size 32×32 pixels, evenly split into 10 mutually exclusive classes (6,000 images per class). It is divided into 50,000 training images and 10,000 test images. Each image has 3 colour channels (RGB), so every image can be represented as a flat feature vector of 32 × 32 × 3 = 3,072 pixel intensity values, in addition to its single class label. Its small size and low resolution make it computationally cheap while still being non-trivial for classification.
